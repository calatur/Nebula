Gear & Techniques

Every shot in the gallery was taken on a phone, not a telescope. Here's the exact settings used for each type of shot, and the workflow that turns a raw file into a finished image.

Settings by Shot Type

Recommended camera settings for common night-sky subjects
Subject Mode Exposure ISO Notes
Star trails Long exposure / stacking 20–30 min (stacked) 400–800 Use a stacking app instead of one huge exposure to control noise.
Milky Way RAW / DNG 10–15 sec 1600–3200 Wider aperture lens performs best; avoid moonlit nights.
Andromeda / deep sky RAW / DNG, tracked 5–10 sec per frame 1600 Stack 20+ frames in Sequator to pull detail out of the noise.
Orion Night mode 3–6 sec 800–1600 Bright enough for a single, unstacked exposure on most phones.

Processing Workflow

  1. Shoot in RAW/DNG wherever the app supports it — JPEG compresses away the detail you need for editing.
  2. Stack multiple frames of the same scene in Sequator to boost signal and cut sensor noise.
  3. Bring the stacked file into Snapseed to adjust contrast, reduce noise further, and correct white balance.
  4. Export at full resolution — resist the urge to over-sharpen; night skies punish heavy-handed editing.

Quick Tips

  • Turn off HDR — it fights long exposures.
  • Use a timer or Bluetooth shutter to avoid camera shake.
  • Check moon phase before planning a shoot; new moon is best.
  • Cold weather drains batteries fast — bring a spare.
